Getting started

Open the Dashboard, pick a problem instance (Augerat A-n32-k5 or the synthetic Dynamic-City-40 grid), choose an algorithm, and press Execute Optimization. The swarm runs client-side; the route map, convergence chart, and tour manifest table update live.

Mathematical formulation

1. Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP)

Given a complete graph $G = (V, E)$ with depot $v_0$ and customer set $V_c = V \setminus \{v_0\}$, customer demands $q_i > 0$, uniform vehicle capacity $Q$, and travel costs $c_{ij}$:

$$ \min \sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{i \in V} \sum_{j \in V} c_{ij} x_{ijk} $$
subject to:
$$ \sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{j \in V, j \ne i} x_{ijk} = 1 \quad \forall i \in V_c \quad \text{(Each customer visited once)} $$ $$ \sum_{i \in V_c} q_i \left( \sum_{j \in V} x_{ijk} \right) \le Q \quad \forall k \in \{1,\dots,K\} \quad \text{(Vehicle capacity bound)} $$ $$ \sum_{j \in V_c} x_{0jk} = 1, \quad \sum_{i \in V_c} x_{i0k} = 1 \quad \forall k \in \{1,\dots,K\} \quad \text{(Depot tour conservation)} $$
2. Quantum Delta-Potential Well (Sun et al. 2004)

In Quantum-Behaved PSO, particles move in a quantum state space bound by an attractive $\delta$-potential well centered at the local stochastic attractor $p_{i,d}(t)$:

$$ p_{i,d}(t) = \phi \cdot P_{i,d}(t) + (1 - \phi) \cdot G_d(t), \quad \phi \sim U(0, 1) $$ $$ X_{i,d}(t+1) = p_{i,d}(t) \pm \alpha \cdot |mbest_d(t) - X_{i,d}(t)| \cdot \ln\left(\frac{1}{u}\right), \quad u \sim U(0, 1) $$

Where the Mean Best position is $mbest(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M P_i(t)$ and the contraction factor $\alpha$ linearly decays from $\alpha_{\max} \to \alpha_{\min}$ over iterations $t_{\max}$.

3. Prins (2004) Optimal Split Decoder

Continuous swarm position vectors are sorted using Largest Order Value (LOV) to produce a giant tour permutation $\pi$. An auxiliary DAG is constructed where directed edge $(i, j)$ represents a valid feasible route from depot $v_0$, serving sequence $(\pi_{i+1}, \dots, \pi_j)$ without exceeding capacity $Q$, with weight equal to route distance. Bellman-Ford/topological shortest path computes the globally optimal partition in $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ time.
