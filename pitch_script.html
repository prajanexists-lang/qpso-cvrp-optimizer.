Part 3: Explaining QPSO — The Mathematical Core Member 2 (Math Lead / You)

[STAGE CUE: Take center stage. Project authoritative mathematical command. Refer to KaTeX equations on screen]

"Thank you. Classical PSO describes particles via Newtonian mechanics with positions $X$ and velocities $V$. But Newton's equations are bounded: if velocity decays, the particle cannot escape a potential well. QPSO, introduced by Sun et al., fundamentally strips away the velocity vector. We model each particle as a quantum state bound inside a delta-potential well centered at a stochastic local attractor $p_{i}$."

Equation 1: 1D Time-Independent Schrödinger Wave Function
$$\psi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \exp\left(-\frac{|x - p|}{L}\right), \quad L = \alpha \cdot |mbest - X|$$
Equation 2: Position Update via Monte Carlo Inverse CDF Sampling
$$X_{i,d}(t+1) = p_{i,d}(t) \pm \alpha \cdot \big|mbest_d(t) - X_{i,d}(t)\big| \cdot \ln\left(\frac{1}{u}\right), \quad u \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$$
Equation 3: Swarm Mean Best Vector (Global Quantum Coherence)
$$mbest(t) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M P_i(t) = \left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M P_{i,1}, \dots, \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M P_{i,n}\right)$$

"Notice the mathematical implication of the probability density function $|\psi(x)|^2$: Particles possess a strictly non-zero probability of appearing anywhere in $\mathbb{R}^n$ at any iteration. Unlike classical PSO, which gets trapped when velocity hits zero, QPSO has an exponential tail that guarantees an infinite domain of exploration."

"To bridge this continuous quantum domain into discrete valid vehicle tours, we apply Largest Order Value (LOV) to map $\mathbb{R}^n \to \Pi_n$, yielding a giant permutation tour. Then, we solve vehicle partitioning not by greedy clustering, but via Prins' (2004) Optimal Split DAG. We construct a directed acyclic graph where each feasible sub-tour is an edge with distance cost, and compute the globally optimal fleet partition in polynomial time $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ using topological Bellman-Ford."

"Now, a critical question arises: Why QPSO over established algorithms like Clarke & Wright's Savings Heuristic, or Large Neighborhood Search?"

"The Clarke and Wright Savings algorithm (1964) is an iconic milestone in operational research. It computes savings $s_{ij} = c_{i0} + c_{0j} - c_{ij}$ and greedily merges routes. But as a deterministic greedy heuristic, Clarke & Wright makes irreversible decisions in the first 5% of its execution. If an early merge is sub-optimal, it locks downstream routes into bad topologies, resulting in a persistent 8% to 15% optimality gap on benchmark instances."

"Meanwhile, state-of-the-art heuristics like HGS-CVRP or ALNS rely on up to 25 hyper-parameters — destroy operators, repair weights, simulated annealing cooling schedules. When deployed in live smart cities with shifting fleet sizes, tuning them is intractable."
