1. Graph-Based Network Modeling (Deliverable 1)

• Directed Multigraph Formulation $G = (V, E, W(t))$:

Road topology is represented as a directed network where $V$ denotes intersections/depots and $E$ denotes directional road segments. Edge weights $W_{ij}(t)$ dynamically scale with real-time BPR saturation ratios:

$$W_{ij}(t) = \frac{d_{ij}}{v_{\text{free}}} \cdot \left[1 + \beta \left(\frac{\text{flow}_{ij}(t)}{\text{cap}_{ij}}\right)^\gamma \right] + \text{Penalty}_{\text{congestion}}(t)$$

• Real-Time Impedance Telemetry Ingestion:

Edge traversal weights update dynamically at 1 Hz from GPS speed traces, flow density sensors, and incident reports, recalculating impedance on the fly.

2. 1D Schrödinger Delta-Well QPSO (Deliverable 3)

• Quantum Bound State Search Mechanics:

Replaces classical Newtonian velocity vectors with bound quantum wavepackets centered at the swarm Mean Best ($m_{\text{best}}$), exploring via Laplace probability clouds:

$$X_{i,d}(t+1) = p_{i,d} \pm \alpha(t) \cdot |m_{\text{best}, d} - X_{i,d}(t)| \cdot \ln(1/u)$$

3. Bottleneck Tunneling vs Local Trap Stagnation

• Classical PSO Trap vs QPSO Tunneling: Classical velocity decay traps particles in local minima during road congestion (Red dot). The double-exponential wavepacket maintains non-zero tunneling probability via $\ln(1/u)$, discovering optimal bypass routes (Green dot).

• Continuous-to-Discrete Permutation Mapping (LOV): Continuous quantum coordinates $X_d$ map to discrete vehicle visit sequences via Largest Order Value (LOV) argsort ranking, ensuring zero illegal sub-tours.

1. Mathematical Optimization Formulation (Deliverable 2)

• Multi-Objective Cost Function & Flow Conservation: Binary decision variable $x_{ijk} = 1$ if vehicle $k$ traverses edge $(i, j)$. Jointly minimizes physical distance $d_{ij}$ and dynamic congestion penalty $W_{ij}(t)$:

$$\min Z = \sum_{k \in K} \sum_{(i,j) \in E} [d_{ij} + \lambda \cdot W_{ij}(t)] x_{ijk} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{i \in V} q_i \le Q, \quad a_i \in [e_i, l_i]$$

• Operational Capacity & Customer Time-Window Bounds: Total customer demand served per vehicle route is capped at payload capacity $Q$. Service arrival times $a_i$ are strictly constrained to time windows $[e_i, l_i]$, penalizing early arrival waits and late delivery delays.

2. Prins DAG Tour Partitioning & Software Stack

• Linear-Time $\mathcal{O}(n \cdot B)$ Prins DAG Dynamic Programming: Splits continuous giant tours into optimal multi-vehicle routes on an auxiliary DAG without illegal capacity or time-window violations:

$$V[j] = \min_{0 \le i < j} \big(V[i] + c(i+1, j)\big) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{m=i+1}^j q_{\pi_m} \le Q \quad [\mathcal{O}(n \cdot B) \text{ Prins DAG DP}]$$

• High-Performance Software Architecture (Deliverable 4): Client-side WebAssembly (WASM) / multi-threaded ES6 Web Workers guarantee sub-second (<850 ms) solver runtime; Next.js & HTML5 Canvas provide live route radar dispatch.
